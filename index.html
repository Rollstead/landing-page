The Problem

Your assessment is an estimate. It is worth checking.

Commercial property owners receive an assessed value each year, but many never independently test whether that value is well supported. When an assessment is too high, the tax impact can continue until the value is corrected.

Most owners do not have time to build the comparison themselves.

Public records exist, but turning them into a useful property-by-property analysis takes time and context.

A high tax bill does not automatically mean a bad assessment.

We look for evidence. If the data does not support an appeal, that is a useful answer too.

The goal is a better decision, not a predetermined conclusion.

Rollstead is designed to help owners focus attention where the numbers indicate a meaningful opportunity may exist.

Evidence First

We look beyond the tax bill.

The question is not simply whether taxes feel high. The question is whether the assessed value is supported by the available evidence.

Assessed market value Current public assessment and classification information.
Assessment history How the property's assessed value has changed over time.
Property characteristics Size, use, class and other relevant physical attributes.
Comparable properties Relevant peer properties and market-value relationships.
Value per square foot A useful comparison point when appropriate for the property type.
Potential financial impact An estimate of what a supported value change could mean in dollars.

Minnesota Deadlines

Do not wait until the last minute.

Minnesota property-assessment appeal deadlines depend on the route you use and the jurisdiction. Local and county review dates can occur earlier and vary by location.

For Minnesota Tax Court, property-tax petitions are generally due by April 30 of the year the taxes are payable. Confirm the exact deadline shown on your valuation notice and the requirements that apply to your property.
